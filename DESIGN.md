# snypd — the site's design brief

This is the **site's** brief, not a theme's. A theme's DESIGN.md says what that look is; this one says
what this site is for and what its owner has already decided about how it should look. A theme built for
this site is built against both.

The `## Taste` log at the bottom is the part that matters most and the part a factory run must read
first (docs/29 §7.3). It is the record of what was refused on sight, in the owner's words, dated. A theme
that repeats something in that log is a round wasted.

## Use scene
Somebody sent here by a Product Hunt post, a Show HN thread or a search for "MCP CMS", on a desktop in
daylight or a phone on a sofa, giving it ten seconds to say what this is before they leave. A second,
smaller audience comes back to read: the log, the release notes, the case study.

## Visitor mode
Persuade, then Read.

## References
The two the product look was measured against (docs/25): the CMS field's home pages — one big sentence,
the install command in the hero, a lot of air — with the discipline of a specimen sheet under it.

## The rut
The SaaS landing kit: three feature cards in a row with icons, a gradient blob behind the hero, a
tracked-caps eyebrow over every section, and Inter.

## Boldness goes here
The front page's one big picture — the film — and the sentence above it. Everything else steps back.

## Safe / Risk
- Safe: one type family, one hairline between sections, and the section heading in a left column.
- Safe: no page names the CMS it is compared to.
- Risk: a front page whose hero is a 45-second film. The cost is the button at 1,200 px, taken knowingly
  (docs/25 §11).
- Risk: a specimen-sheet register on a marketing page. The cost is that it reads as documentation to
  somebody skimming; the sentence and the film carry the ten seconds instead.

## Chosen
- `taste.eyebrow`: none on this site. The rut names it and the front page does not need one.

## Taste

The owner's verdicts, in the owner's words, newest first. A line goes in here when a look is refused or
picked on sight — not when a rule is agreed in prose. Every entry is dated and names the session.

- **2026-09-18 · S35 · the hero, nine rounds, decided on sight.** What stands: *one centred column — the
  sentence, the film, the button, the lede, the window.* What was refused: the lead beside the button
  (*"the half beside the sentence was empty — the most important part"*), the window beside an 80 px
  sentence (*"the pacing was wrong"*), the wordmark under the window. Recorded as decision 210, which
  replaced a left-set hero the brief had already approved in prose.
- **2026-09-18 · S35 · the hero copy is shorter than the brief wanted.** Cut on Sunny's word: the
  sentence naming themes, plugins and content types, and *Launching 6 October 2026*. The launch date is
  nowhere on the front page. The capabilities are carried by the band below, and no page names the CMS
  they are compared to.
- **2026-09-18 · S34 · a look is picked from a picture, before it is a theme.** Two standalone mockups of
  the front page, no renderer, photographed at 1440 and 390; Sunny picked B. Decision 209 — and the
  reason for it is that the two looks before it (docs/17, docs/24) were approved in prose and refused on
  sight. For *theme* work decision 221 amends this: three rendered candidates, not a mockup.
- **2026-09-18 · S33 · no boxes inside boxes.** The fault the console look had at every band. A card is
  for something that is a card; a list item is a list item.
- **2026-09-18 · S33 · too much text in mono.** Sunny, on the built console page: *"the home page has too
  much text."* The same words that read fine in a sans are a slab in a face where every glyph is one
  width. The rule this left: **a mono theme's front page is measured in lines, not words, and the copy is
  written to that, not before it.**
- **2026-09-17 · U10 · the look is the owner's, on sight.** Decision 209's corollary: a stripe, a tint or
  a neutral that ships is held as shipped until Sunny looks at it and says otherwise. The lint records
  the choice; it does not overrule it.
