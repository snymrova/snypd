Ferrule started in 2017 with two people, a borrowed mill and a tableware brief nobody else wanted because it came with a kiln. We took the kiln. Nine years on we are nine people and the kiln is still here.

The name is the small metal band that holds a brush to its handle. It is the part nobody looks at and the part that makes the tool work. That is the part of a product we like.

## What we believe

A product is not finished when the drawing is approved. It is finished when the factory can make a thousand of it and the thousandth is as good as the first. So we design the object and the way it gets made in the same room, at the same time, and we build the fixtures ourselves.

:::callout{kind="note" title="We keep the drawings, you keep the tools"}
Every jig, gauge and test rig we build for a project is yours when the project ends. The drawings stay with us, so the next studio you hire can get them from us and not from a drawer.
:::

## The floor

The workshop is one floor of a former textile mill on Rua do Almada. Half is machines, half is benches, and the drawing room is upstairs where the light is.

## How a project goes

:::flow{caption="From a first message to the first batch. Most projects go straight down; the loop is the one we are proud of."}
steps:
  - You write to us with the thing, not a brief
  - We visit where it is used
  - ask: Is it a thing we should make?
    yes: Two-week look, then a fixed quote
    no: We say so, and suggest who
  - id: proto
    do: Draw, prototype, break it on the rig
  - ask: Did it survive?
    yes: Tool the fixtures and gauges
    no: { then: proto }
  - First batch, with us on the line
:::
