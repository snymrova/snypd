Grog is clay that has already been fired, ground to a sand and mixed back into raw clay. It opens the body, so water leaves it evenly and it shrinks less. Too little and a large flat piece warps in the kiln; too much and it tears on the wheel and feels like sandpaper on the lip.

## What we changed at Alcobaça

The pottery's stoneware body is theirs and it is good. For the Bitácora plates, the widest piece in the line, we asked for the grog to go from 8 % to 12 % by weight and the grade to come down one sieve, so the same weight of grog is finer. The plates stopped warping and the bowls still throw.

## What we did not change

Everything else. A clay body is a relationship between a pottery and a quarry, and the only reason to touch it is a number you can show. Ours was eleven warped plates in a batch of two hundred, and then none.

:::callout{kind="tip" title="If you are specifying grog"}
Say the percentage by weight and the mesh size, not "medium". Two potteries' medium is a sieve apart.
:::
