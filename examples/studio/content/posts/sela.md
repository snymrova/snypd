:::tldr
Kavel is a joinery in Paredes that wanted its own chair rather than other people's. The design is eleven parts and no metal. The product is the jig: one fixture that holds every joint at the right angle, so the chair comes out the same in ash, oak or walnut, from whoever is at the bench.
:::

## Eleven parts

Two back legs, two front legs, four rails, two back slats and a seat. Every joint is a loose tenon, every tenon is cut on the same router with the same bit, and every part is oriented in the jig with the grain running the way the load runs.

:::callout{kind="note" title="Why no metal"}
A chair with screws is a chair with a repair. A loose tenon in the right glue is stronger than the wood around it, and when a chair finally breaks it breaks in the wood, where the joiner can see it.
:::

## The jig

A jig is a fixture that holds the parts so the tool meets them at the same angle every time. Ours holds all eleven parts of a Sela at once, so the assembly is one glue-up and one set of clamps, and it takes eleven minutes.

:::steps{title="One chair, one glue-up" time="11 min"}
1. **Load** — eleven parts into the jig, grain arrows facing up. The jig will not close if a part is backwards.
2. **Glue** — twenty tenons, one bottle, a minute and a half.
3. **Clamp** — the jig's own cams; no bar clamps.
4. **Wait** — forty minutes, during which the other bench is loading the next one.
5. **Sand and oil** — the only step that takes longer for walnut.
:::
