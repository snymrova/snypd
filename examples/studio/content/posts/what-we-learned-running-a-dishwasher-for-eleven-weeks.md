We built a rack, put twelve pieces in it and ran the studio's dishwasher five times a day for eleven weeks. The glaze story is in [the case](/work/kiln-to-table/). This is what the machine taught us that the colorimeter did not.

## The detergent matters more than the temperature

We started with the caustic detergent Bitácora's kitchens use and switched to a domestic one for a week to see the difference. At the same 65 °C the domestic one did almost nothing to the tenmoku in a hundred cycles. The caustic one browned it in forty. A test that does not use the customer's detergent is not a test.

## Chips come from the rack, not the wash

Every chip we logged was on a rim, and every one of them was on a piece that had been racked against another. We changed the rack so nothing touches, and logged no chips for the last six weeks. The kitchen's racks are now the same shape.

## Write the count on the piece

By week four nobody could tell which bowl had been through how many cycles. A number in underglaze on the foot solved it, and the production pieces carry a batch number in the same place for the same reason.

:::callout{kind="note" title="The rack is on the floor"}
It lives beside the kiln. If you want to test a piece against a commercial wash, ask, and bring your own detergent.
:::
