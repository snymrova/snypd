:::tldr
Marés is a forty-seat café on the Foz waterfront with a terrace that gets the Atlantic wind. We designed the counter, the terrace furniture and the sign. The owner's first question was where the dishwasher goes, so that is where we started.
:::

## Starting at the back

A café's counter is a machine with a person inside it. Ours is 4.2 m long and every station on it, from the grinder to the pass, is where the barista's hands already were when we watched them work in the old place for a week.

## The terrace

The terrace faces west and the wind comes off the sea from about four in the afternoon. The old chairs blew over. Ours are steel, 6.8 kg each, powder-coated in the orange of the sign, and fold flat so the staff can stack forty of them in the two minutes before a squall.

## The sign

The name is set in a face Marta drew for the job, heavy enough to read at forty metres across the coast road. The letters are folded steel, lit from inside, and the only colour on the building.

::figure{src="/media/mares-bulbs.webp" alt="Caged filament bulbs hanging at different heights in front of a café window" caption="Inside, the light is caged filament bulbs, the same cage as the terrace railings."}

## What we would do again

:::faq
### Why steel chairs by the sea?
Because they are heavy, and heavy is the whole point. The coating is a marine grade and the staff wipe them with fresh water at close. Two winters on, no rust.

### How long did it take?
Fourteen weeks from the first visit to opening night, of which the counter was six and the chairs were the rest. The sign was one afternoon and a crane.

### Did you design the menu?
No. We set it, in the sign's face, and gave the kitchen the file. What is on it is theirs.
:::
