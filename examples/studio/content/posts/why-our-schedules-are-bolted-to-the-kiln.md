Every glaze we hand over comes with a firing schedule, and the schedule is not in a PDF. It is etched on a steel plate and bolted to the kiln.

## Because the PDF is on someone's laptop

The celadon on the Bitácora line only works fired to cone 10 with a two-hour hold. A faster schedule crazes it at cycle 60 in the dishwasher. When we handed the recipe over, the pottery's kiln had a schedule taped inside the lid from the previous job, and the laptop with ours was in the office. The plate went on the next week.

## Because a kiln is a machine, and a machine gets a label

Our own CNC machines carry their feeds and speeds on a card in the door. The kiln is the same kind of thing: a process with numbers that must be repeated by whoever is standing in front of it at six in the morning. The numbers belong on the machine.

## What is on the plate

The cone, the ramp in degrees an hour, the hold, the cooling, and one line in capitals: DO NOT SHORTEN THE HOLD. The line is there because someone will.
