:::tldr
Bitácora runs three restaurants and wanted its own tableware. The hard part was not the shape. It was a glaze that keeps its colour through a commercial dishwasher, and a kiln schedule a small pottery could repeat. We found both, and wrote the schedule on the kiln.
:::

## The brief

Bitácora's kitchens break about forty plates a month, which is normal, and replace them with whatever the supplier has, which is not. The owner wanted twelve pieces that a pottery in Alcobaça could make in batches of two hundred, that stack, and that look the same after a year of service as on the first night.

We said yes on the condition that the first thing we designed was the test, not the plate.

::figure{src="/media/kiln-wheel.webp" alt="Hands centring a lump of clay on a spinning wheel, seen from above" caption="Throwing the first bowl. Every shape was thrown by hand before it was turned on the lathe for the mould." width="wide"}

## The test

A commercial dishwasher runs at 65 °C with a caustic detergent. We built a rack that holds twelve pieces and ran it in the studio's own machine, five cycles a day, and measured the glaze with a colorimeter every twenty-five cycles.

The tenmoku was the one everyone liked on the first day. It went brown.

:::callout{kind="warning" title="A glaze is a recipe, and a recipe is a kiln"}
The winning celadon only wins if it is fired to cone 10 with a two-hour hold. On a faster schedule the same recipe crazes at cycle 60. So the schedule is part of the product, and it is printed on a steel plate bolted to Bitácora's kiln.
:::

## The pieces

::figure{src="/media/kiln-hands.webp" alt="Two pairs of hands wedging a lump of grey clay on a wooden bench" caption="Wedging at the Alcobaça pottery. The clay body is theirs; we changed only the grog."}

:::steps{title="From wheel to batch" time="11 weeks"}
1. **Throw** — one of each piece on the wheel, by our ceramicist, to find the wall thickness that stacks.
2. **Turn** — the master shapes on the lathe in plaster, 3 % oversize for shrinkage.
3. **Mould** — two-part slip moulds, twenty of each, so a batch of two hundred is one week of casting.
4. **Fire** — bisque at cone 06, glaze at cone 10 with the hold, on the schedule we wrote on the plate.
5. **Test** — twelve pieces from every batch go through our rack for fifty cycles before the batch ships.
:::

## Questions the kitchens asked

:::faq
### Can we get more colours?
Yes, in the order they pass the rack. Two more celadons are at cycle 250 as this is written.

### What if a piece chips?
It is stoneware fired to cone 10, so it chips less than what it replaced. The pottery keeps a shelf of every shape and ships replacements within the week.

### Why not porcelain?
Porcelain would have been thinner and whiter and would have cost twice as much per piece to fire. The brief said batches of two hundred from a pottery in Alcobaça, and that pottery has a gas kiln that likes stoneware.
:::
